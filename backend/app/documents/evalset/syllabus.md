# Thermodynamics II — Course Syllabus

Lecturer: Dr Amira Hassan. Lectures are on Mondays and Wednesdays from 10:00 to 11:30 in Hall B. Office hours run on Thursdays from 14:00 to 16:00 in room 3.12, and students may also book a slot by email with at least one day's notice.

# Learning outcomes

By the end of the course you should be able to apply the first and second laws of thermodynamics to open and closed systems, calculate entropy changes for real processes, analyse power and refrigeration cycles, and use property tables and Mollier charts with confidence. The course assumes you passed Thermodynamics I and are comfortable with basic calculus.

# Weekly topics

Week 1 reviews energy balances. Weeks 2 and 3 cover entropy, the Clausius inequality and isentropic efficiency. Week 4 is exergy and second-law efficiency. Weeks 5 and 6 cover the Rankine cycle with reheat and regeneration. Week 7 is the Brayton cycle and gas turbines. Week 8 covers vapour-compression refrigeration and heat pumps. Weeks 9 and 10 are psychrometrics and air conditioning. Week 11 is combustion and chemical reactions. Week 12 is revision.

# Assessment

The final exam is worth 60 percent of the grade and is closed book, although a formula sheet is provided. Two problem sets are worth 15 percent each. A laboratory report on the heat pump experiment is worth 10 percent. Late problem sets lose 10 percent per day and are not accepted after three days.

# Exam details

The final exam is three hours long and takes place in the main examination hall. It covers every topic from weeks 2 to 11, with particular weight on cycles and entropy. Bring a non-programmable calculator and your student card. Steam tables are printed at the back of the exam paper.

# Reading list

The core text is Cengel and Boles, Thermodynamics: An Engineering Approach, 9th edition, chapters 7 to 15. A recommended extra text is Moran and Shapiro, Fundamentals of Engineering Thermodynamics. Lecture slides are posted on the course page after each session.
